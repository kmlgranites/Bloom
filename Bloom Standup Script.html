Meet Bloom.

A walkthrough script — what to say, and what to click, as you take the team through the prototype.

● Speaker notes · just read down the page
1 Open · set the scene

The thirty-second hook

Okay — so for the last few weeks I've been building out Bloom, and today I want to actually walk you through the whole thing end to end. Not slides. The real prototype.

Here's the one-line version: job hunting is a part-time job nobody applied for. You find a role, you re-type the same details into yet another form, you write a cover letter you'll never reuse, and you do that forty, fifty times before anything sticks. It's soul-crushing, and honestly most of it is copy-paste.

Bloom's whole bet is this: you should make the decisions, and let our copilot do the typing.

▶Have the prototype open on the Home screen before you start talking. Don't click yet — just let it sit there.
2 Why it matters · the ROI

Before I click anything — the numbers

Let me ground this in why it's worth our time. The average serious job seeker spends somewhere around fifteen to twenty minutes per application once you count finding it, tailoring the answers, and filling the form. Multiply that across a real search and it's dozens of hours of pure admin.

Here's what Bloom changes:

So the pitch to a user is simple and honest: we give you back your evenings. Instead of grinding forms, you're spending ninety seconds approving an application that's already written in your voice — or zero seconds, if you let it run on auto.

The ROI line to land

"If Bloom saves someone even ten hours over a job search — and it saves a lot more than that — then a few dollars a month is a no-brainer. We're not selling software. We're selling their time back."

— breathe, then start the demo —
3 Onboarding · the easy setup

"It learns you in two minutes"

Let me start where the user starts. The biggest risk with a product like this is a setup wall — a giant form before you get any value. So we did the opposite.

▶Trigger onboarding: sidebar avatar → Settings → Account → "Replay guided setup." (Or just have it pre-loaded.)

Meet Sprout — our copilot. Notice onboarding isn't a form, it's a conversation. Sprout introduces itself and the very first thing it says is "tap, don't type."

Every question is a tap. Your role, your experience level, your salary floor, the kind of companies you want. Four quick chapters. And while you're tapping, Sprout is quietly reading your résumé in the background and pre-filling everything it can — so by the time you're done, it already knows your years of experience, your authorization status, your notice period.

The point: zero blank text boxes. You're never staring at a cursor wondering what to write.

▶Tap through 2–3 chapters so they see how fast it moves. Point at the animated Sprout on the left as you talk.

By the end of this, Sprout has a profile of you. That's the foundation for everything else — because now it can fill any application as if it were you.

4 Home · the daily landing

"Here's your morning in one screen"

▶Land on Home. Gesture to the queue and the weekly stats.

This is what you see when you open Bloom each day. Up top, the stats that matter — applications out, replies in, time saved. We lead with time saved on purpose, because that's the emotional payoff.

Below that is your queue — roles Sprout found and drafted overnight, ready for you. And there's a recap of what Sprout did while you were away: "found this," "skipped four roles below your salary floor," "a recruiter replied." It's transparent. You always know what your copilot has been up to.

From here there are two ways to work, and this is the core of the product — so let me show both.

5 Review mode · you approve each one

"Ninety seconds, all taps"

The default is Review mode — Sprout drafts, you approve. This is for people who want to stay in control of every send.

▶From Home, click "Review →" on a queued job (Linear). Let the review screen load.

So here's a real application. On the left, it's a conversation, not a form. Sprout walks you through each question one at a time. Cover letter? It's already drafted — I can tap "looks good," or tap "make it warmer," "make it shorter," and it rewrites instantly. Watch.

▶Click a refine pill like "Make it warmer." Show the rewrite, then click "Perfect, next."

Then the structured stuff — years of experience, salary expectation, work authorization, visa. These are the questions every application form asks, and they're already filled in from your profile. You just confirm. Tap, tap, tap.

On the right, two things I love. There's a checklist showing exactly what still needs your eyes versus what's auto-handled — so you're never guessing how much is left. And there's "Bloom handled the rest," which lists everything it auto-filled, so there's no black box.

And if you ever want to go off-script —

▶Click the floating Sprout bubble (bottom-right). Type "how does my fit break down?" and send.

— there's a chat. But it's scoped on purpose. It answers things about the role, tweaks an answer, or helps you skip. It's not an open-ended chatbot that wanders off — it's a focused assistant. Ask it about your fit, the hiring manager, the interview process, and it answers.

The whole thing takes about ninety seconds. Then you hit "Approve & send," and it's gone — submitted in your voice.

▶Finish the questions, then click "Approve & send." Note the confirmation. Optionally show the Skip dialog first.
6 Auto mode · hands-free

"Or… don't lift a finger"

Now — that's for people who want control. But some people just want results. So there's a second gear: Auto Apply.

▶In the sidebar Sprout card, flip the toggle to 🚀 Auto. Point out it now reads "LIVE · FULL AUTO" with the green pulse.

One switch. Now Sprout doesn't wait for you — it finds matching roles, fills them, and submits them automatically. You'll notice the copilot card lights up green: live, full auto. It's working for you in the background.

And here's the nice part — it's still transparent and still reversible.

▶Go to Applications. Show the first column now reads "Ready to send" with the "One-Click Apply to all" button and the "Sending in ~Xm" badges.

Everything queued up shows right here. I can let it run on its own schedule, or — if I'm impatient — I hit "One-Click Apply to all," and it fires off the entire batch at once.

▶Click "One-Click Apply to all." Let the batch overlay play through — jobs ticking to ✓ — to the "applications sent 🎉" summary.

Look at that — it's applying to each one, in your voice, ticking them off, and at the end it tells you exactly how much time it just saved you. Twelve applications. A couple of hours of form-filling. Gone.

Review mode for control. Auto mode for scale. Same copilot — the user just chooses how much to hand over.

7 Applications · the tracker

"And nothing falls through the cracks"

Last piece. Once applications are out, they live here, on the Applications board. It's a simple pipeline: Review → Applied → Replied → Interviewing → Offer.

▶On the Applications board, hover a card and click the eye icon to open the job detail modal.

Tap any application and you get the full picture — the role, why Sprout matched you, and a complete history timeline: when it was drafted, when it was submitted, when the recruiter viewed it, when they replied. Every application has a paper trail.

▶Open the History tab in the modal. Then close it and show the move menu (the arrow icon) → mark one as Selected.

And as things move, you just update the status — drag it forward, or use the move menu to mark it Interviewing, Selected, or not selected. It's your whole search in one organized place, instead of a spreadsheet and twelve browser tabs.

8 Close · bring it home

The wrap

So that's Bloom, start to finish. Two-minute setup that feels like a chat. A copilot that drafts every application in your voice. Review mode when you want control, Auto mode when you want it hands-free. And a tracker so nothing slips.

The thing I'd ask you to hold onto is the ROI, because that's the story: we're giving people back the hours job hunting steals from them. Every other feature is in service of that one promise.

Decisions stay with the human. The busywork goes to Sprout.

That's the demo — happy to take questions, or run any part of the flow again live.
